## Full Content

### Overview & Background

Bristol Farms is described as "the low-key Southern California grocery chain born on the Palos Verdes Peninsula in 1982." The chain was "founded in 1982 in Rolling Hills Estates by Irv Gronsky and Mike Burbank."

Currently, Bristol Farms "counts 13 locations, with a 14th slated to open in Hollywood in mid-April."

### Company Strategy & Positioning

Steve Howard, the chain's senior vice president of retail, stated: "We're the Disneyland of food." The article notes this "neatly sums up the company's focus on discovery and prepared-food theater."

Good Food Holdings owns Bristol Farms along with Lazy Acres and other specialty banners. According to the holding company, "Bristol Farms' model blends gourmet groceries with chef-led prepared foods and robust floral and bakery departments."

### Pricing & Product Offerings

- "The Cookie" is a warm, 6-ounce item "made with imported Callebaut chocolate and sold for $4"
- Smoothies and bottled juices "generally start around $9"
- These are "notably under similar items at Erewhon"

Dan Evon (vice president of culinary) and Joyce de Brevannes (vice president of marketing) emphasized they are "doubling down on in-house kitchens, sushi counters, and seasonal juice programs."

### Store Locations & Expansion

Bristol Farms operates in "affluent suburbs from Newport Beach to Palm Desert and into Los Angeles neighborhoods." The new Hollywood location targets "theater crowds and tourists streaming down Hollywood Boulevard."

### Competitive Positioning

Where Erewhon emphasizes "scarcity, sleek branding, and celebrity partnerships," Bristol Farms offers "utility and breadth: smoked meats, made-to-order sushi, a full bakery, and floral arrangements."
