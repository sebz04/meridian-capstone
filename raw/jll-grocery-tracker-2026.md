### The barbell shopper has arrived

**Food price inflation impact:**
- Food costs at home have increased over 30% since early 2020
- Headline inflation cooled to around 2.7%
- Nearly 80% of shoppers report noticing price increases at supermarkets — more than any other retail category

**Consumer bifurcation:**
- High-income households experiencing wealth growth, spending on premium wellness products
- Middle- and lower-income families facing squeezed budgets, drained savings, rising credit card debt
- Market growth concentrating at both extremes; the middle is being squeezed

### Private label is no longer a backup plan

**Store brand growth:**
- Private-label products surged 30% since 2021
- Store brands account for over 21% of all grocery spending
- Private label spending reached a record $282.8 billion
- Nearly half of shoppers surveyed actively choose store brands over name brands to save money

**Shopping behavior changes:**
- Consumers making more frequent, shorter shopping trips
- Strategy shift from weekly big shops to "bite-sized trips" for budget management
- Consumers cherry-picking deals across multiple stores

**Kroger confirmation:** Kroger publicly confirmed that "middle-income households in particular are using these bite-sized trips as a budget management strategy."

### The great grocery divergence is underway

**Traditional grocery market share decline:**
- Traditional supermarket foot traffic share dropped from 75.1% (2019) to 73.2%
- Despite seeming modest, represents billions in lost sales for a large industry

**Winners identified — two distinct categories gaining share:**
1. Value-focused operators
2. Fresh-format specialty grocers

**Value player expansion (Aldi):**
- Opened 180 new stores in 2025
- Plans another 180 new stores for 2026
- Part of an aggressive multi-year expansion

**Foot traffic growth comparison (same-store visits):**
- Kroger: 0.3% (essentially flat)
- Trader Joe's: 10.4%
- Whole Foods: 9.8%
- Aldi: 8.3%

### Canada confirms the broader trend

- Loblaw concentrated on No Frills banner in Ontario and Alberta; Maxi in Québec
- Empire expanding FreshCo operations in Western Canada
- Metro focusing on Super C expansion in Québec and Food Basics in Ontario
- Canadian store food inflation tracks above the official Consumer Price Index
- Parallel trend confirms the barbell consumer dynamic is "broader North American phenomenon, not a U.S.-specific quirk"

### Real estate fundamentals remain strong, but nuance matters

**Grocery-anchored property performance:**
- Vacancy rate: 4.0% (vs. 6.3% for non-anchored centers)
- NNN rent premium: 4.4%

**Pandemic resilience (historical comparison):**
- COVID period: net absorption negative for four consecutive quarters; new deliveries plummeted 68%; rents still grew 1.5%
- Great Recession: availability surged from 6.2% to 8.2%
- Pandemic: availability increased less than 1 percentage point before tightening

**Current market conditions:**
- Availability compressed to 4.7%
- Leasing velocity slowed as quality space becomes scarce
- Recent negative net absorption in 2025 reflects broader retail closures, not grocery-specific weakness

### Geography matters more than ever

**Regional concentration (2025):**
- Southeast captured 215 of 400 tracked new store openings
- Florida leads, driven by population migration, business-friendly policies, strong consumer spending

**Secondary opportunity cluster:**
- Mid-Atlantic region (New Jersey, Maryland, Washington D.C./Baltimore corridor)
- High-density, affluent markets
- Operators filling gaps and upgrading existing locations rather than pure greenfield growth

### The physical store is evolving, not disappearing

**Online grocery projection:**
- Total online grocery sales projected to reach $228 billion by 2030
- Represents over 23% of total grocery spending

**Home delivery surge:**
- Home delivery now accounts for 45% of online grocery orders
- Has surpassed both pickup and shipping options

**Real estate implications — properties need to accommodate:**
- Delivery driver staging areas
- Efficient traffic flow for multiple vehicles
- Flexible layouts handling both customer shopping and order fulfillment simultaneously

### Investors love grocery stores, but asset acquisition is getting competitive

**Investment activity (2025):**
- Transaction volume surged 42% to nearly $11 billion
- Institutional investors increased share of acquisitions to 27% — highest level in over a decade

**Cap rate compression:**
- Cap rates fell approximately 40 basis points from the cyclical peak in 2023
- Averaged 6.7% nationally at close of 2025

### The bottom line

The future belongs to properties anchored by grocers capturing visit growth: value players serving budget-conscious households and fresh-format operators serving wellness-focused consumers. "Evaluate your assets and pipeline not just on whether they have a grocery anchor, but on whether they have the right one for today's bifurcated market."
