# Grocery Market Trends — ICSC 2026

**Source:** ICSC Exchange, John Egan, April 24, 2026  
**Raw file:** [raw/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md](../../raw/icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)

## Key takeaways for Meridian

**Specialty grocers are the bright spot.** Specialty format grocers like Trader Joe's show 3% YoY growth and strong traction with Gen Z. Meridian's positioning — prepared foods, local sourcing, smaller footprint — maps to this winning segment. This is tailwind, not headwind.

**Differentiation is now table stakes.** JLL's Jaggi: "The days when any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading." Meridian's distinct identity (specialty, community-anchored) is a competitive asset, but it must be legible to shoppers. The loyalty program is one lever.

**Consumer mix is shifting toward grocery.** 36% of consumers increased grocery shopping while reducing restaurant visits. This is a structural opportunity for prepared-foods-heavy operators like Meridian.

**"Clear reason to be loyal" drives retention.** Consumer Edge: shoppers gravitate toward "unbeatable value at a hard discounter or a curated private-label experience." Meridian's loyalty data is underused — this framing suggests the 40K-member base is an untapped retention asset.

**SoCal competitive note — Gelson's.** Gelson's launched "ReCharge" micromarkets (3,000–5,400 sq. ft.) combining premium grocery with EV charging. Direct Los Angeles competitor experimenting with a format Meridian hasn't pursued. Worth monitoring.

**Investment context for Pasadena.** Grocery-anchored retail deals hit ~$11B in 2025 (+42% YoY). Blackstone acquired 130 properties for $6.7B. Landlords are actively seeking grocery anchors — Meridian may have negotiating leverage on leases.
