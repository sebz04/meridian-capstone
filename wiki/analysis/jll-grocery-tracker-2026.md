# Grocery Tracker 2026 — JLL

**Source:** JLL, James D Cook / Heli Brecailo / Keisha Virtue / Monica Mason, February 26, 2026
**Raw file:** [raw/jll-grocery-tracker-2026.md](../../raw/jll-grocery-tracker-2026.md)

## Key takeaways for Meridian

**Meridian sits in the winning "fresh-format" bracket.** JLL identifies two categories gaining share — value operators (Aldi: 8.3% same-store visit growth, 180 new stores in 2025 and again in 2026) and fresh-format specialty grocers (Trader Joe's: 10.4%, Whole Foods: 9.8%) — while traditional mid-market chains stall (Kroger: 0.3%). Meridian's prepared-foods, local-sourcing identity places it in the fresh-format camp, the stronger of the two growth categories by visit growth.

**The "barbell" consumer split gives a testable hypothesis for uneven store performance.** Food-at-home prices are up 30%+ since early 2020; middle-income households are squeezed and drained while high-income households keep spending on premium/wellness. Growth is concentrating at both extremes, and the middle is being hollowed out. Meridian's slower stores are worth checking against this pattern — do they sit in more "middle," less differentiated trade areas?

**Private label has real economic weight behind it, not just anecdote.** Store brands hit a record $282.8B in 2025, over 21% of all grocery spend, up 30% since 2021 — and nearly half of shoppers actively choose store brand to save money. This corroborates the ICSC framing (see [ICSC Grocery Formats 2026](icsc-grocery-formats-2026.md)) that a "curated private-label experience" is one of two things that earn loyalty, and gives it hard numbers.

**Grocery-anchored real estate is tight — and getting more competitive to acquire, not just to lease.** Vacancy sits at 4.0% (vs. 6.3% non-anchored) and availability has compressed to 4.7%. The 2025 investment volume figure (~$11B, +42%) independently corroborates the ICSC article's figure. New detail: institutional investors' share of acquisitions hit 27%, the highest in over a decade, while cap rates compressed ~40bps to 6.7% nationally — more capital chasing the same grocery anchors means more competing bidders for Meridian, not just eager landlords.

**Most current expansion activity is concentrated outside Southern California.** Of 400 tracked 2025 store openings, 215 were in the Southeast (Florida leading); a secondary cluster is emerging in the Mid-Atlantic. This doesn't argue against Pasadena, but it means Meridian would be expanding against the regional grain rather than riding a wave — worth understanding the specific Pasadena hypothesis, since it isn't where national growth capital is currently flowing.

**Delivery/fulfillment is now a real estate design variable, not an afterthought.** Home delivery is 45% of online grocery orders (has surpassed pickup); online grocery is projected to reach 23%+ of total spend by 2030. Any new Meridian site, including Pasadena, should plan for delivery staging and fulfillment flow alongside in-store shopping.
