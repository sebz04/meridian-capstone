# Loyalty Program

## Known (from brief)

- ~40,000 members
- Purchase history available
- Has not been meaningfully analyzed by Meridian to date
- Dana identifies this as an underused asset

## Market context (from ICSC 2026)

**Loyalty is now a competitive differentiator.** Consumer Edge: shoppers gravitate toward retailers that give them "a clear reason to be loyal, whether that's unbeatable value at a hard discounter or a curated private-label experience." For a specialty grocer like Meridian, the loyalty program is the mechanism for delivering that curated experience — if the data is actually used.

**Traditional chains are losing loyalty.** Declining loyalty among shoppers at Publix and Safeway signals an opening for specialty operators who invest in the relationship. Meridian's 40K-member base, currently untapped, is a defensible asset.

## Market context (from JLL 2026)

**Private label has become a mainstream loyalty lever, with real numbers behind it.** Store-brand spending hit a record $282.8B in 2025, over 21% of all grocery spend, up 30% since 2021 — and nearly half of surveyed shoppers say they actively choose store brands to save money. This corroborates the ICSC framing above that a "curated private-label experience" is one of two things that earn loyalty (the other being hard-discount pricing). Meridian's 40K-member loyalty base is the natural channel to test a private-label push, if it doesn't already have one.

## Open questions

- What data is captured per transaction — amount only, or also items and categories?
- What is the enrollment rate relative to total customer volume?
- Is there any segmentation or tier structure in the program?
- What do members buy differently than non-members — is this tracked?
- Does loyalty enrollment or visit frequency vary by store?
- Does Meridian have (or plan) any private-label product line? Is loyalty data being used to identify demand for one?
