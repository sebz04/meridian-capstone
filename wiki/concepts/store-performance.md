# Store Performance

## Known (from brief)

- 14 stores; performance is uneven — some took off quickly, others were slower
- Leadership currently judges performance on instinct and spreadsheets
- Dashboard goal: sales performance by store and by category

## Market context (from ICSC 2026)

**Consumer spending is shifting to grocery.** 36% of consumers increased grocery shopping in 2026 while reducing restaurant visits. Prepared-foods operators like Meridian are positioned to capture this shift — but only if execution is strong.

**Specialty format is outperforming.** ~3% YoY growth for specialty grocers; traditional chains declining in loyalty, especially among lower-income shoppers; discounters plateauing. Stores that underperform relative to this trend are worth investigating.

**Loyalty is the differentiator.** Consumer Edge: shoppers gravitate to "unbeatable value at a hard discounter or a curated private-label experience." Performance variation across Meridian's 14 stores may partly reflect how well each location has built a loyal base — making the 40K-member loyalty dataset a useful diagnostic.

**Differentiation matters more than ever.** JLL: "The days when any grocery store could anchor a center equally well are fading." Slower stores may be underperforming because their local positioning isn't sufficiently distinct.

## Market context (from JLL 2026)

**The "barbell" consumer split may explain uneven store performance.** Food-at-home prices are up 30%+ since early 2020; middle-income households are squeezed while high-income households keep spending on premium/wellness. The result is a bifurcated market where value operators (Aldi: 8.3% same-store visit growth, 180 new stores in both 2025 and 2026) and fresh-format specialty grocers (Trader Joe's: 10.4%, Whole Foods: 9.8%) are both gaining share, while a flatter traditional middle (Kroger: 0.3%) stalls. Meridian's prepared-foods, local-sourcing identity places it in the fresh-format winning category — worth checking whether Meridian's slower stores skew toward more "middle," less differentiated trade areas.

**Private label is now a proven performance lever, not just a differentiation theory.** Store brands reached 21%+ of grocery spend ($282.8B), up 30% since 2021; nearly half of shoppers actively choose store brand over name brand to save money. This gives the private-label angle (see [Loyalty Program](loyalty-program.md)) real weight as a performance driver.

## Market context (from Hoodline, Bristol Farms 2026)

**A regional peer is proving the "prepared-food theater" strategy drives volume.** Bristol Farms — a SoCal specialty grocer roughly Meridian's size — is beating the ultra-premium Erewhon on foot traffic with "utility and breadth" (in-house kitchens, made-to-order sushi, full bakery, floral) rather than scarcity or celebrity branding. SVP Steve Howard: "We're the Disneyland of food." This is independent, market-level validation that prepared-foods-as-differentiator (Meridian's own strategy) drives performance, not just theory from national trend pieces.

**Bristol Farms is priced as accessible-premium, not discount.** "The Cookie" at $4, smoothies/juices from ~$9 — "notably under" Erewhon but nowhere near hard-discount pricing. If the JLL "barbell" pattern above is real, this middle-premium lane should be getting squeezed — worth watching whether Bristol Farms' growth holds, as either evidence the barbell isn't absolute or an early warning for any Meridian store leaning toward this same positioning.

## Open questions

- What metrics does Meridian currently track per store, formally or informally?
- What is the revenue spread across stores — which are top and bottom performers?
- Is there a correlation between store age, square footage, neighborhood type, and performance?
- How is "category" defined in the POS — what categories exist?
- Do loyalty program enrollment rates differ across stores? Do they correlate with performance?
- Do Meridian's higher- vs. lower-performing stores differ by neighborhood income mix, consistent with the "barbell" pattern?
- Does Meridian carry a private-label line today? If not, is that a gap relative to the value/differentiation trend?
- How does Meridian's prepared-foods program compare to Bristol Farms' — depth of in-house kitchen, sushi, bakery, floral?
